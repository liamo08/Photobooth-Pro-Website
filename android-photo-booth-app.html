What You Need

An Android Tablet
Is the Whole Booth

The tablet is the camera, the screen and the computer. Everything else on this list is optional and plugs into its USB port.

Tablet or Phone

Any device on Android 7.0 or later with Google Play. A 10-inch or larger tablet on a stand suits a booth. A phone works for roaming shots or as a spare.

USB-C Hub (Optional)

Needed only if you add a printer or a camera. Use a powered hub so the tablet charges while the printer or camera is plugged in. The tablet's USB-C port must support USB host mode, also called OTG.

Dye-Sub Printer (Optional)

DNP, Citizen or HiTi, cabled by USB. The app sends the print itself, so there is no driver to install and no laptop sitting behind the booth.

Sony Camera (Optional)

For sharper photos than a tablet camera, tether a Sony camera by USB in PC Remote mode. See Sony photo booth software →

Set Up

From Google Play
to First Photo

About ten minutes the first time. Do it at home on Wi-Fi, before the event.

  1. Install BoothLedger Photo Booth from Google Play.It is a free download for Android 7.0 and later.
  2. Create your account in the app.That starts the free 30-day trial with every feature. No card is needed. If you already use BoothLedger on Windows or iPad, sign in with that account.
  3. Create an event and pick a template.Choose a ready-made design for a wedding, birthday or corporate event, or build your own in the template editor.
  4. Allow the camera, then take a test photo.Android asks for camera permission the first time. New events start in test mode, so practice shots stay out of the guest gallery.
  5. Plug in the printer, if you have one.Android asks whether BoothLedger may use the USB device. Tick the option to always open BoothLedger for it, then print the test photo.
  6. Switch the event to live when guests arrive.Photos upload to the online gallery as they are taken, or later if the venue has no signal.

Printing & Cameras

Printers and Cameras
That Work on Android

Android has no drivers for event printers, so the app speaks to them directly over USB. This is the full list of what it supports today.

Dye-sub printers over USB

DNP DS620, DS820, DS-RX1, QW410, DS40, DS80 and DS80DX. Citizen CW-01 and CW-02. HiTi P520L and P525L. 4×6 prints and 2×6 strips come out full-bleed.

Other printers

Wi-Fi printers and home inkjets are not supported on Android. Send prints to a sharing station instead, or run the booth on Windows, which prints to anything with a Windows driver.

Built-in cameras

Tablets start on the front camera so guests see themselves. Phones start on the back camera, which is usually the better one, and you can switch either way.

Sony cameras over USB

Live view on the tablet and full-resolution photos from the camera. Tested with a Sony ZV-E10 II on a Google Pixel 8. Canon tethering is on iPad and Windows.

FAQ

Android Photo Booth
Questions Answered

Is there professional photo booth software for Android?

Yes. BoothLedger Photo Booth is on Google Play for Android tablets and phones running Android 7.0 or later. It runs the whole guest flow on the touchscreen: live view, countdown, photos, GIFs, boomerangs and video, print templates, and sharing by QR code, email or text. It is the same BoothLedger that runs on Windows and iPad, signed in with the same account, so it is built for paid events and not only for a party at home.

Which Android tablets and phones does it work on?

Any tablet or phone on Android 7.0 or later that has Google Play, which covers current Samsung Galaxy Tab, Lenovo Tab and Google Pixel devices. For a booth on a stand, a 10-inch or larger tablet gives guests a comfortable screen. If you plan to cable a printer or a camera, pick a device with a USB-C port that supports USB host mode (often called OTG), and use a powered USB-C hub so the tablet keeps charging during the event.

Can an Android photo booth print photos?

Yes. BoothLedger prints straight from the Android device to a dye-sublimation printer over a USB cable. There is no print server, no PC and no Wi-Fi involved. The first time you plug the printer in, Android asks whether BoothLedger may use it. Tick the box to always open BoothLedger for that printer and it will not ask again. Prints can also be sent to a separate sharing station.

Which printers work with the Android app?

Over USB: DNP DS620, DS820, DS-RX1, QW410, DS40, DS80 and DS80DX, Citizen CW-01 and CW-02, and HiTi P520L and P525L. Android has no system printer drivers for these, so BoothLedger talks to the printer itself. Home inkjets and Wi-Fi printers are not supported on Android. If you need one of those, use BoothLedger for Windows, which prints to any printer with a Windows driver.

Can I connect a DSLR or mirrorless camera to an Android tablet?

A Sony camera, yes. Set the camera to PC Remote, plug it into the tablet or phone by USB, and BoothLedger shows live view and takes full-resolution photos through it. We tested this with a Sony ZV-E10 II on a Google Pixel 8. Canon tethering is available on iPad and Windows, and Nikon on Windows. Without a tethered camera the app uses the device's own front or back camera.

Does the Android app work without Wi-Fi?

Yes, once the event is on the device. Open the event once while you are online so its templates and settings are saved to the tablet. At the venue the booth keeps taking and printing photos with no connection. Uploads to the online gallery and any email or text shares wait in a queue and send when the connection returns.

Is the Android photo booth app free?

The app is free to download and every new account gets a free 30-day trial with every feature and no card needed. Photos taken during the trial carry a watermark. After the trial, plans start at €12 a month for Basic and €35 a month for Pro, which adds AI styles, AI video and integrations. You can subscribe on boothledger.com or inside the app through Google Play. See pricing.

Can I use the same account on Android, iPad and Windows?

Yes. One BoothLedger account signs in on Android, iPad, iPhone and Windows, and one subscription covers all of them, with two devices included. Events, templates and galleries are stored in BoothLedger Cloud, so you can build an event on a laptop and open it on the Android tablet at the venue.

How do I stop guests leaving the app on an Android tablet?

Two things together. In BoothLedger, set an operator PIN so settings and the exit are locked behind it. In Android, turn on app pinning (Settings, then Security) and pin BoothLedger, which stops the home and recent-apps buttons leaving the app until you unpin it. BoothLedger keeps the screen awake by itself.
