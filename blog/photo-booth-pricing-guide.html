Photo Booth Pricing Guide 2026: How Much to Charge for Events

May 23, 2026 12 min read

Pricing is the single most-asked question by new photo booth operators. Charge too low and you grind through weekends for sandwich money. Charge too high and your enquiries die before they reach a deposit. The pricing in the photo booth market has shifted noticeably in 2025–2026 as AI effects, mirror booths and trading-card workflows pushed the premium end up while the iPad-only "kiosk in a stand" end commoditised.

This is the working operator's guide, not the SEO-fluff version. Below are real ranges by event type, region, hours and add-ons — pulled from the rates we see on contracts and quote sheets in Ireland, the UK, US and Australia in 2026. By the end you'll have a defensible price for any enquiry that lands in your inbox.

The simple test: if a corporate client asks for a quote and you'd be happy to take a wedding at the same price, your corporate quote is too low. Corporate budgets are 1.5–2x weddings for the same booth on the same day.

Two takeaways. First, the premium tier always exists in any market — even rural Ireland has wedding clients paying EUR 900+ for a quality booth. Second, the gap between entry and premium is at least 2x. If your pricing is bunched in a EUR 50 range, you're missing the top half of the market.

The pricing structure that converts best in 2026 is three tiers, named for the value not the cost. We've seen this convert 35–55% better than a single-price quote on the same total spend.

Software (BoothLedger, prorated) EUR 1–2

Attendant labour (if hired, ~EUR 18/hour + setup) EUR 90–120

Add insurance, booking platforms, marketing, accountancy and other fixed costs allocated per event and you're at around EUR 220–320 of real cost on a 4-hour booth. A EUR 350 wedding booking is barely profitable. A EUR 650 wedding booking is genuinely profitable. A EUR 950 premium booking is what makes the business work.

Common pricing mistakes

1. Pricing against Gumtree-tier competitors

If you compare your rates to the EUR 250 "uncle with an iPad" listings you'll race to the bottom. The clients you want aren't on that page — they're booking through wedding planners, venues and corporate event managers. Price for that audience.

2. Single-price quoting

One price gives a client a binary yes/no decision. Three packages turn the decision into "which package," which is a far easier yes to land.

3. Underpricing midweek & off-season

If you offer a 50% discount on a Wednesday, you train clients to ask for a 50% discount on a Saturday. Discount sparingly and only with a structural reason (multi-event booking, charity, off-season).

4. Quoting without seeing the venue

"Upstairs venue with no lift, 90 minutes setup carry" should not be the same price as "ground-floor venue, ten metres from the van." Quote based on actual logistics.

5. Not charging for branded design

Custom template design is two billable hours of skilled work. Don't throw it in. Most clients will happily pay EUR 75–100 for a branded template if you list it as a line item.

6. Forgetting travel

A booking 90km away is two extra hours of driving and EUR 40–60 in petrol. If you don't charge it explicitly, you absorb it from your day rate.

How to raise rates without losing bookings

Three tactical moves used by operators who went from EUR 450 to EUR 750+ per wedding inside 18 months:

  1. Add value before raising price. Add AI background removal, trading-card prints, or a glam booth tier. Once the new value exists, the price increase is justified.
  2. Raise once a year on January 1st. Existing booked clients keep their rate; new enquiries pay the new rate. Most clients don't push back if the increase is visible against new features.
  3. Filter your enquiry form. If you ask for budget on the enquiry form and decline the lowest-budget enquiries politely, you stop wasting quoting time and stop normalising low rates in your head.

Frequently asked questions

How much should I charge for a photo booth rental?

In 2026, the typical photo booth rental in Ireland and the UK runs EUR 450–900 for a wedding (4 hours, prints, unlimited photos). Corporate events charge EUR 600–1,500 for the same window. In the US, equivalent pricing is USD 550–1,200 for weddings and USD 750–2,000 for corporate. Premium DSLR + AI rigs charge 30–50% above these midpoints.

What does a photo booth rental include in 2026?

A standard photo booth rental package in 2026 includes a 3–4 hour run time, unlimited photos, on-site dye-sub prints, digital copies by SMS/email/QR, an attendant, basic props, and a branded or themed template. Premium packages add AI effects, green screen, scrapbook guestbook, USB of all photos, and idle-hour add-ons.

How much is a wedding photo booth?

Wedding photo booths typically run EUR 450–900 in Ireland and the UK, USD 550–1,200 in the US, and AUD 750–1,400 in Australia for a 4-hour DSLR booth with prints. Pricing rises with hours, premium effects (AI, glam booth, mirror booth), branded templates and travel.

How long should a photo booth be at a wedding?

Three to four hours is the sweet spot for a wedding photo booth. That covers cocktails through to the first half of the dance floor, when guest demand peaks. Anything under 2 hours feels rushed; anything over 5 sees diminishing usage as the dance floor takes over.

How do I price photo booth add-ons?

Common add-on pricing in 2026: extra hour EUR 75–150, scrapbook guestbook EUR 75–120, custom-branded template EUR 50–100, USB of all photos EUR 50–80, idle hour EUR 50–75, travel beyond 30km EUR 1–2 per kilometre, AI effects upgrade EUR 80–150, premium booth type (mirror, glam) EUR 150–300 upgrade over standard.

Is photo booth rental a profitable business?

Yes. A booth operator running 30–50 paid weekends per year at average EUR 600 per event grosses EUR 18,000–30,000 from a single rig that cost EUR 3,000–5,000 to build. Operators with two or three rigs and a small team scale to EUR 80,000–200,000 a year. Software is a low fixed cost — BoothLedger plans start at EUR 20 per month.
