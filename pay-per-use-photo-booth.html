Four Ways Your Booth
Connects to the Venue

An unattended booth needs more than a camera and a printer. It has to take the money, put on a show and tell you what happened. BoothLedger does all three from the same Windows app you already run.

Pay to Play Beta

Charge for every session. Guests scan a QR code and pay on their phone, or use a Nayax card reader or a coin acceptor. The booth waits until the session is paid for, and credits come back automatically if a session is cancelled before the first photo. The money goes to your own account.

Lights & Effects

Triggers switch things on at moments in a session: an LED strip goes bright white on the countdown, a relay fires a bubble machine on each photo, Home Assistant dims the room. Presets for WLED, Shelly and Home Assistant. Works without internet.

Webhooks

BoothLedger sends each session moment to Zapier, Make, your CRM or your own server, including the guest’s share link when a photo uploads. Signed, retried for about a day if your server is down, and every delivery is logged.

Control API

A local API on the booth for venue systems, a Stream Deck or a phone shortcut: check status, start a session, lock the booth between bookings and add paid credits. Token-protected, and each payment id only ever counts once.

How It Works

Pay to Play,
Step by Step

Pay to play is in beta. Set it up and test it at home or in the office before you put it in front of the public.

  1. Set the price. Turn on Charge for sessions and choose how many credits a session costs, on the booth or on BoothLedger for the whole event.
  2. Choose how guests pay. No hardware: connect your Stripe account on BoothLedger and the booth shows a QR code guests pay on their phone. Or put a Nayax VPOS Touch in pulse mode, or use a coin or note acceptor, and wire its pulse output to a pulse-to-USB keyboard adapter that sends F13. Each pulse adds one credit.
  3. The guest pays. The booth shows the QR code and/or “Tap your card to start”. Once the payment arrives, the start screen opens.
  4. The session runs. Credits are taken when the session really starts. If it’s cancelled, fails or the app closes before the first photo, the credits go straight back.
  5. Everything is recorded. Each credit and session can fire a trigger on the venue network and a webhook from BoothLedger, so your lights, sheets and reports keep up on their own.

Built Secure

Connected Without
Leaving the Door Open

A booth in a public venue is on someone else’s network, in front of strangers. The integrations were built with that in mind.

Signed Messages

Webhooks and signed triggers carry an HMAC-SHA256 signature and a timestamp, so your server can prove a message came from your booth and reject old ones replayed later.

Locked-Down API

The control API needs a token, refuses calls from web pages and listens only on the booth PC until you choose to open it to the venue network.

Safe Webhook Targets

Webhooks only go to public https addresses, never into a private network. Signing secrets are stored encrypted and can be rotated at any time.

Fails Closed

If the booth can’t confirm your plan, for example while offline, pay to play stays on. A dropped connection never turns a paid booth into a free one.

FAQ

Pay-Per-Use Photo Booth
Questions Answered

What is a pay-per-use photo booth?

A pay-per-use photo booth, also called a self-service or unattended booth, runs without an attendant and charges guests before each session. Guests walk up, start a session on the touchscreen, take their photos and get them printed or sent to their phone. BoothLedger adds what an unattended booth in a venue needs: payment before each session, lights and effects that react to the session, and a record of every session sent to your own systems. For the install itself, see permanent photo booth installs →

Can guests pay by card at the photo booth?

Yes, with pay to play, which is in beta. A Nayax VPOS Touch card reader in pulse mode connects to the booth PC through a pulse-to-USB keyboard adapter. Each successful payment adds credits, and the booth shows “Tap your card to start” until a session is paid for. Test it before you rely on it at a live venue.

Can guests pay by QR code without a card reader?

Yes, in beta. Connect your own Stripe account on BoothLedger and the booth shows a QR code on screen. Guests scan it and pay on their phone with Apple Pay, Google Pay or a card, and the session starts as soon as the payment arrives. The money goes to your Stripe account. BoothLedger keeps 3.7% of each QR payment and Stripe’s own fees apply. QR payments need internet, are switched off in test mode, and a payment the booth doesn’t use within 15 minutes is refunded automatically.

Does it work with a coin or note acceptor?

Yes. Any coin or note acceptor with a pulse output can feed the booth through a pulse-to-USB keyboard adapter set to send F13. One pulse is one credit, and you choose how many credits a session costs.

What happens if a guest pays and the session fails?

Credits are only taken when the session really starts, and they are given back automatically if the session ends before the first photo, whether it was cancelled, hit an error or the app closed. The balance is saved on the booth PC, so a restart loses nothing and the next guest tap starts a session. Pulse mode is one-way, so the card payment itself is not voided automatically.

What does BoothLedger charge on payments?

QR code payments through Stripe: BoothLedger keeps 3.7% of each payment, and Stripe’s own fees apply. Card reader and coin payments go straight to you: card payments land in your own Nayax merchant account, Nayax charges its own fees and you need a Nayax account. Pay to play itself is part of the AI Photobooth plan.

Can the photo booth control lights, smart plugs or effects?

Yes. Triggers make the booth call devices on the venue network at moments in a session, such as session start, countdown, photo taken, print and session end. There are presets for WLED LED controllers, Shelly relays and Home Assistant, and any other web address works too. The booth calls the device directly, so triggers keep working without internet.

Can BoothLedger send sessions and photos to Zapier, Make or our CRM?

Yes. Webhooks from BoothLedger send each moment to any https address, including Zapier and Make. A photo uploaded webhook includes the guest’s share link, so you can log photos to a Google Sheet, post to Slack or add a CRM record. Failed deliveries retry for about a day and every delivery is listed so you can resend it. See also the photo booth CRM →

Is there a photo booth API?

Yes. The booth runs a local control API that venue systems can call with a token: read the booth’s status, start a photo, boomerang or video session, lock and unlock the booth, and add paid credits with a unique payment id so a payment is never counted twice. It listens only on the booth PC unless you turn on network access.

Are photo booth integrations secure?

Every webhook and signed trigger carries an HMAC-SHA256 signature so the receiver can prove it came from your booth. The control API needs a token, refuses requests from web pages and only listens on the booth PC by default. Webhooks only go to public https addresses and their secrets are stored encrypted. If the booth cannot confirm your plan, for example while offline, pay to play stays on rather than giving sessions away.

Can I run a photo booth at a Santa’s grotto or Christmas market?

Yes. Charge per session at the booth by card or coin, or let staff add a free session from the booth’s settings for families who paid at the till. Triggers can switch fairy lights or a smart plug on the countdown, and families get their photo printed and on their phone by QR code.

Does a self-service photo booth work offline?

Yes. Pay to play, triggers and the control API all run on the booth itself, so they work without internet. Photos and moments are sent to BoothLedger when the connection comes back, and webhooks go out from there.
